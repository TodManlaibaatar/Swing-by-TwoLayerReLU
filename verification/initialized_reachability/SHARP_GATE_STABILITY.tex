\section{A sharper one-sided gate bound}
Let $y=(u_i,w_i)_i$, $F$ be the empirical ReLU gradient field, and
$e_y(x)=x-f_y(x)$. For a radius $R$, define the potentially crossing samples
$\mathcal N_i=\{n:|u_i^Tx_n|\le R\|x_n\|\}$ and
\[
 C_i=\frac1N\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_i}\|e_y(x_n)\|\|x_n\|,
 \qquad K_i=\frac1N\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_i}\|x_n\|^2,
 \qquad D=\|y\|+R/2.
\]
Use reference-only positive conormal weights
\[
 B_i(s)=\frac1N\sum_{|u_i^Tx_n|\le s\|x_n\|}
                 (w_i^Te_y(x_n))_+\|x_n\|.
\]
Suppose $B_i(s)\le a_i+\beta s$ for $0\le s\le R$, and the residual
Hessian matrices on smooth pieces of this ball have norm at most $A$.
Then, for compatible selectors and $\|z\|\le R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-gate-stability}
 \langle z,F(y+z)-F(y)\rangle
 \le (A+\beta+\Gamma)\|z\|^2+\|(a_i)_i\|_2\|z\|,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \Gamma=\tfrac12\max_i(C_i+DRK_i)
       +D\left(\sum_i\|w_i\|^2K_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
In particular parameter perturbations need not be charged independently by
$R$ inside every neuron's positive gate weight.

\begin{proof}
On each smooth piece $DF=-J^*J+H_e$; retain the nonpositive term and bound
the residual-Hessian term by $A\|z\|^2$ along the parameter segment.
At a gate crossing on this segment the contribution to the scalar pairing
is at most
\[
 \frac{|z_{u_i}^Tx_n|}{N}
                 (w_i(s)^Te_{y+sz}(x_n))_+.
\]
The reference term gives $\sum_i\|z_{u_i}\|B_i(\|z_{u_i}\|)$.
Write $r=\|z\|$. ReLU Lipschitzness along the parameter segment gives
$\|f_{y+sz}(x_n)-f_y(x_n)\|\le Dr\|x_n\|$.
The other conormal terms are bounded by
\[
 \|z_{w_i}\|\|e_y(x_n)\|
 +\|w_i\|Dr\|x_n\|+\|z_{w_i}\|Dr\|x_n\|.
\]
After summing over possible crossings their contribution is at most
\[
 \sum_i(C_i+DrK_i)\|z_{u_i}\|\|z_{w_i}\|
 +Dr\sum_i\|w_i\|K_i\|z_{u_i}\|
 \le\Gamma r^2.
\]
Use $2ab\le a^2+b^2$, Cauchy--Schwarz, and $r\le R$.
The reference weights contribute at most $\|(a_i)\|r+\beta r^2$.
Endpoint partial selectors and simultaneous crossings satisfy the same bound.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Signed enclosure of the residual matrices.}
A second improvement avoids adding a full absolute near-gate contribution
to a matrix that already contains it. At a reference midpoint put
$c_{n,ab}=e_y(x_n)_a x_{n,b}$. For an uncertain gate set $\mathcal N_i$
and the certainly active set $\mathcal F_i$, the matrix computed with
reference residual and actual selectors belongs entrywise to
\[
 \left[\frac1N\sum_{n\in\mathcal F_i}c_{n,ab}
       +\frac1N\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_i}\min(0,c_{n,ab}),\quad
       \frac1N\sum_{n\in\mathcal F_i}c_{n,ab}
       +\frac1N\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_i}\max(0,c_{n,ab})\right].
\]
Residual change costs at most $\lambda D_{\rm out}$ in operator norm if
$\|f_\theta-f_y\|_n\le\sqrt\lambda D_{\rm out}$ and
$\|\mathbb E_n xx^T\|\le\lambda$. This follows by Cauchy--Schwarz,
retaining the signed coordinate intervals before taking a matrix norm.
Both estimates are deterministic inequalities. The initialized interval
validator implements them with outward bounds; its floating-point
feasibility prototype is separate and is not used as a proof premise.
